## Rules (6)

### Give dense or expressive pages a visible map
<!-- rule:hierarchy.give-dense-pages-a-map -->
`hierarchy.give-dense-pages-a-map` · principle · confidence **medium** · evidence: 3 independent ref(s) + 1 standard(s) · provisional

**When a page carries many items or long sections, give readers a persistent map — numbered chapters, a sticky index, colour-coded entry tiles or content-type forms — so expressive presentation never hides structure.**

- **WHEN:** content_density ∈ {high}; page_goal ∈ {information-consumption, storytelling, brand-expression}
- **CONSIDER:** numbered chapter index that becomes sticky; consistent visual form per content type; colour assigned per section
- **VERIFY:** the map is keyboard reachable and labelled (nav landmark); current section indicated; colour is not the only cue (WCAG 1.4.1)
- **AVOID WHEN:** single-message landing pages
- **TRADE-OFFS:** sticky indexes consume horizontal space on tablets
- **WHY (inferred):** A 150-update release keeps a chapter index visible beside every scene; a brand-guidelines hub uses colour tiles as section entry points; an Arabic news page encodes content type by card form. INFERRED: structure substitutes for scale when the type scale is narrow or the art direction is loud.
- *Evidence:* supported by ref-002 (Shopify Editions — The Renaissance Edition (Winter '26)), ref-004 (Dropbox Brand), ref-011 (Megaphone) · standards: wcag22. Details: `npm run why -- hierarchy.give-dense-pages-a-map`.

### When visitors arrive with a task, the hero is a router
<!-- rule:hierarchy.router-hero-for-task-intent -->
`hierarchy.router-hero-for-task-intent` · principle · confidence **medium** · evidence: 3 independent ref(s) · provisional

**If most visitors come to do or find something specific, make the first viewport a router — a search field, department choices or top tasks — instead of a slogan.**

- **WHEN:** page_goal ∈ {task-completion, information-consumption, conversion}; content_density ∈ {medium, high}
- **CONSIDER:** search field as the hero's primary action; department or category buttons over imagery; a short list of most-requested tasks right below
- **VERIFY:** router visible without scrolling at 390; keyboard reaches it first after skip link; labels are explicit, not clever
- **AVOID WHEN:** brand launch, storytelling or single-product pages; pages where the visitor does not yet know what to choose
- **TRADE-OFFS:** less room for brand storytelling above the fold
- **WHY (inferred):** A public portal (search), a data publication (search + popular topics) and a luxury department store (department buttons over photography) all spend the first viewport on routing. INFERRED: intent is already formed, so persuasion copy delays the task.
- *Evidence:* supported by ref-007 (GOV.UK), ref-008 (Our World in Data), ref-012 (Ounass). Details: `npm run why -- hierarchy.router-hero-for-task-intent`.

### Cap the content container; let only backgrounds and media bleed
<!-- rule:layout.cap-content-width -->
`layout.cap-content-width` · principle · confidence **medium** · evidence: 5 independent ref(s) · provisional

**Keep content containers capped (≈ 960–1300 px) and identical at 1440 and 1920; use full-bleed only for backgrounds, imagery or deliberate art direction.**

- **WHEN:** page_goal ∈ {any}
- **CONSIDER:** max-inline-size on a centred container; full-bleed bands behind a capped inner column
- **VERIFY:** container width at 1440 vs 1920 (verify-page); line lengths at 1920 do not exceed the reading-measure rule
- **AVOID WHEN:** immersive canvases and galleries whose content is the image
- **TRADE-OFFS:** large empty margins on ultra-wide screens
- **WHY (inferred):** Five references hold the same container width at 1440 and 1920 (990, 1280, 1266, 1180, 798 px); the references that grow to ~1500–1800 px are illustration- or image-led.
- *Evidence:* supported by ref-007 (GOV.UK), ref-008 (Our World in Data), ref-009 (Stripe), ref-012 (Ounass), ref-004 (Dropbox Brand) · counterexamples: ref-010 (Tabby) — illustration-led page grows to 1824 px at 1920; ref-011 (Megaphone) — image grid grows to 1546 px. Details: `npm run why -- layout.cap-content-width`.

### Expose the grid when the subject is a system
<!-- rule:layout.expose-the-grid-for-systems -->
`layout.expose-the-grid-for-systems` · principle · confidence **medium** · evidence: 3 independent ref(s) · provisional

**Making the grid visible (hairlines, modular cells) works when the content is itself a system — brand guidelines, type specimens, infrastructure — and reads as decoration elsewhere.**

- **WHEN:** page_goal ∈ {brand-expression, information-consumption}; content_density ∈ {low, medium}
- **CONSIDER:** visible cell lines in a pale tint; content snapped to cells; empty cells as rhythm
- **VERIFY:** grid lines below 3:1 contrast so they do not compete with text; mobile cell sizes keep text readable
- **AVOID WHEN:** commerce listings; long-form reading; dashboards with their own data grid
- **TRADE-OFFS:** visual noise if the grid competes with content
- **WHY (inferred):** A brand-system site, a payments-infrastructure site and a type specimen expose their grids; each subject is about systems and structure.
- *Evidence:* supported by ref-004 (Dropbox Brand), ref-009 (Stripe), ref-005 (Exat Typeface). Details: `npm run why -- layout.expose-the-grid-for-systems`.

### Structure dense layouts with hairlines, borders and space
<!-- rule:layout.structure-with-lines-not-shadows -->
`layout.structure-with-lines-not-shadows` · principle · confidence **medium** · evidence: 5 independent ref(s) · provisional

**In dense or information-led layouts, separate content with hairlines, thin borders, background bands and spacing; avoid stacking soft shadows, glows and large radii on every container.**

- **WHEN:** content_density ∈ {medium, high}
- **CONSIDER:** 1 px dividers and borders; square or small-radius (0–8 px) containers; tinted background bands for grouping
- **VERIFY:** verify-page signatures: rounded-card-grid, glow-accents absent or justified; grouping is still perceivable without colour
- **AVOID WHEN:** playful illustrated brands may use large radii deliberately (record the reason)
- **TRADE-OFFS:** can feel austere without strong typography or imagery
- **WHY (inferred):** Information-led references measure zero coloured glows and few large radii; structure comes from rules, borders, visible grids and bands.
- *Evidence:* supported by ref-007 (GOV.UK), ref-008 (Our World in Data), ref-009 (Stripe), ref-012 (Ounass), ref-004 (Dropbox Brand) · counterexamples: ref-010 (Tabby) — large-radius image cards are part of a deliberately playful illustrated identity. Details: `npm run why -- layout.structure-with-lines-not-shadows`.

### At 390 px, transform priorities instead of shrinking the desktop
<!-- rule:responsive.transform-not-shrink -->
`responsive.transform-not-shrink` · principle · confidence **medium** · evidence: 6 independent ref(s) + 1 standard(s) · provisional

**Design the mobile composition deliberately: drop or condense secondary content, move secondary controls behind a button, reframe art direction, and turn menus into task sheets — rather than scaling the desktop layout down.**

- **WHEN:** device ∈ {mobile-first, responsive-general}
- **CONSIDER:** shorter headline variant on mobile; side panels → button + sheet; stats → one line; footers → accordions; crop/reframe imagery per viewport; pinned primary CTA in the mobile menu
- **VERIFY:** first viewport at 390 shows the primary action; nothing essential only reachable on desktop; menu: aria-expanded toggles, Escape closes, focus moves in
- **AVOID WHEN:** already single-column, text-first pages, where stacking is sufficient
- **TRADE-OFFS:** two compositions to maintain; risk of hiding content mobile users need
- **WHY (inferred):** Six references change content and interaction at 390: trimmed headline and pinned CTAs, panel-to-button chart controls, rotated specimen word, reframed 3D scene, accordion footer, menu that doubles as storefront.
- *Evidence:* supported by ref-009 (Stripe), ref-008 (Our World in Data), ref-005 (Exat Typeface), ref-006 (See What Eye See — Eye Condition Simulator), ref-012 (Ounass), ref-003 (Scout Motors) · counterexamples: ref-007 (GOV.UK) — stacks without transforming — works because the content is already a single task-first column · standards: wcag22. Details: `npm run why -- responsive.transform-not-shrink`.
