The work

I. The idea

Every six minutes, a tide gauge in the Tagus estuary takes a reading. Tidewrack turns each one into sound: the height of the water sets the pitch, the speed of its rise and fall sets the density of notes, and slack water, the still minutes when the tide turns, becomes silence.

II. The room

Forty speakers hang through a converted warehouse on the waterfront. There is no stage and no front row. You walk through the composition, and it moves past you from speaker to speaker, the way water finds its way along a channel.

III. The composer

Inês Varela was born in Setúbal, on the Sado estuary, and works with environmental data. For Tidewrack she wrote the rules, not the notes. For six weeks, the river writes the rest.

10,560 readings between 14 November and 27 December, one every six minutes, day and night. No two days of the score are the same.

Low-tide sessions

On four evenings the room reopens at 20:30 for a performance close to low water, when the score thins into silence. The tide keeps its own time, so the silence arrives at a different minute each night.
