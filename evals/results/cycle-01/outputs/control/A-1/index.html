The work

The idea

Every six minutes, a tide gauge in the Tagus estuary records the height of the water. Tidewrack turns each reading into sound: the height sets the pitch, the speed of the rise or fall sets the density, and the slack water at high and low tide becomes silence. The tide never quite repeats, so neither does the piece.

  • Height →becomes pitch
  • Speed →becomes density
  • Slack water →becomes silence

The space

Forty speakers hang at different heights inside Armazém 7, a converted warehouse on the Cais do Sodré waterfront. There is no stage and no front row. As the water rises and falls outside, the sound gathers and drains through the room: walk through it, or sit and let it pass over you.

The composer

Inês Varela is a composer born in Setúbal, on the Sado estuary. She works with environmental data, treating measurements as a kind of notation: she writes the rules, and the water writes the music.

Low-tide sessions

On four evenings the warehouse reopens after hours for a performance timed to low water. As the estuary drains, the music thins toward silence, then returns when the tide turns.
