How it becomes sound
A

The idea

Written by the tide

Every six minutes, a tide gauge in the Tagus estuary takes a reading. Tidewrack turns each one into sound: the readings are mapped to pitch, density and silence, so the piece never repeats and is never finished. For six weeks, the river writes the score as it goes.

  • Pitch

    The height of the water. High tide sounds high; low water sinks to the bottom of the stave.

  • Density

    The speed of the tide. When it runs, notes crowd together; as it slows, they thin out.

  • Silence

    Slack water. When the tide turns and the river stands still, the room falls quiet.

B

The space

Performed by the room

Forty speakers hang from the roof of Armazém 7, a converted warehouse on the Lisbon waterfront. There is no stage and no front row. You walk through the sound, and the music shifts around you as the water shifts outside.

C

The artist

Inês Varela

Inês Varela is a composer, born in Setúbal on the Sado estuary, who works with environmental data. She treats measurements as scores that are already written and only need a room in which to be heard. In Tidewrack, the score is the Tagus.

Evening performances

Low-tide sessions

Four evening performances timed to the turn of the tide, when the water and the score sink to their lowest. Each begins at 20:30. Next to each date is the predicted time of low water.
